Global Alcohol Consumption

Exploring worldwide drinking patterns, trade flows, and gender-based drinking proportions; importing and exporting alcohol products across specific countries, including teenage drinking behaviours in studies and time management aspects.

Global Consumption Overview

Alcohol consumption varies dramatically across the globe. South America and North Africa consistently have high levels of beer consumption, while Europe consumes mostly wine and beer. Spirits are mainly consumed in Asia. These patterns are often linked to cultural and religious factors.

The visualization suggests that most parts of the world prefer beer, while spirits and wine have lower consumption ratios, respectively.

Country & Gender Consumption Breakdown

The percentage of alcohol consumption in Australia, Canada, Thailand, the United Kingdom, and the United States is shown in a bar graph comparison, with an interactive selection box for three types of drinks: beer, wine, and spirits. Australia and the United Kingdom have similar patterns, where wine and beer consumption are relatively stable. On the other hand, Thailand and the United States show similar patterns, where wine consumption is low but spirits consumption is high. Canada shows a relatively balanced consumption ratio across all three types of drinks, with slightly higher beer consumption.

The drinking gender ratio in selected countries across each region, typically Asia, is relatively balanced, where consumers are mostly both female and male, with a slight leaning toward females in most countries. However, Malaysia and the Netherlands show a slightly larger proportion of male consumers.

Global Alcohol Trade

Exporting and importing alcohol show the industry dynamics in this field across different countries and their areas of expertise. Among the selected countries, Thailand, the United States, Australia, and the Netherlands are mainly exporting alcohol, while Canada engages in both importing and exporting. The United Kingdom is primarily an importing country with some exporting activity.

Alcohol Import & Export by Country

Among the selected countries, Canada shows the largest proportion of alcohol imports, while the other countries have similar proportions in the stacked area chart.

Canada and the United States have similar proportions of alcohol exports, while the United Kingdom has a much lower value in the dataset.

The United Kingdom has lower export values in the dataset than Canada and the United States, making it less visible in the chart. Thailand's export value is not specified in the dataset, making it not visible in the chart.

Australia: Consumption Over Time From 2011 to 2018

This heatmap shows Australian drinking patterns from 2011 to 2018, which are mainly focused on beer and wine, while spirits, ready-to-drink beverages, and cider are less in demand.

Beer obviously has become less popular over the years.

This donut chart illustrates Australian drinking patterns over time, focusing on five main categories of alcoholic beverages: beer, wine, spirits, ready-to-drink beverages, and cider.

Area chart below elaborates on the heatmap, showing the popularity of each type of drink over the years.

Student Alcohol Consumption

Among teenagers aged 15 to 22, the bar chart indicates that most recorded drinking cases occur at ages 15 to 18, which are minors. Both female and male are represented, with a slightly higher proportion of females.

This radar chart elaborates on ages 15 to 18, showing the mean values of students across various aspects, including health, going out, failures, study time, and absences, on a scale from 1 to 5 (1 = low and 5 = high).
